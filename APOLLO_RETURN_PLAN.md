# Apollo-inspired return flight plan

Keep the surface mission short: find parts, restore solar power, and earn the rover for field trips, then return to the crashed ship at base to repair and launch it. The main co-op sequence begins after boarding. Use the existing ship interior and give each event a visible symptom, a diagnosis at a station, and a shared recovery action.

1. **Departure: Apollo 12 telemetry outage.** Apollo 12 was struck by lightning after Earth launch. Switching the Signal Conditioning Equipment to auxiliary power restored usable telemetry. In the Mars game, an electrical transient can make readings unreliable at departure. One player switches the telemetry system to auxiliary power while another verifies that the readings stabilize. [NASA technical memorandum](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240011265/downloads/12-3-24%2020240011265.pdf)
2. **Cruise: Apollo 13 oxygen and power loss.** A service module oxygen tank ruptured. The crew reduced power use and relied on the lunar module as a lifeboat. Use NASA's accident timeline to build the event: read the oxygen and voltage changes, isolate the damaged system, and move life support to backup power together. [NASA accident chronology](https://www.nasa.gov/history/detailed-chronology-of-events-surrounding-the-apollo-13-accident/)
3. **Approach: Apollo 11 guidance overload.** The 1201 and 1202 alarms happened during lunar descent. The computer preserved essential guidance work. Adapt that to Earth approach: one player identifies the overload and drops the competing workload while the pilot holds the entry course. [NASA Apollo 11 landing journal](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a11/a11.landing.html)

Show a short debrief after each recovery that names the original mission, phase, and actual crew response. The Mars-to-Earth sequence combines three separate historical events into one fictional flight. Keep the station tasks playable solo, and let co-op players divide diagnosis, repair, and piloting.
